# RelayHQ beyond ITSM — external customer service and training

*Dated 2026-08-16. Companion to the ITSM / project-management / automation market synthesis of the same date in this directory. Where that document asks "is RelayHQ a credible internal service-management tool", this one asks the two questions that decide whether RelayHQ is a **category** or a **feature**: does it hold up as external customer service, and does the training layer survive contact with the evidence.*

This synthesis was assembled on 2026-08-16 from four parallel research passes — external customer service platforms, LMS and course authoring, customer education / customer academies, and single-source content architecture with short-form visual instruction — each of which was then independently fact-checked against primary sources and had its failed claims stripped out. "Verified" here means a named person fetched the primary artifact and read the field, the enum or the sentence: Intercom's 707 KB OpenAPI 2.11 spec and Front's 500 KB core-api spec were downloaded and grepped directly; Gorgias's `llms.txt` object index was enumerated; Moodle's plugin directories and language files were read through the GitHub API against `moodle/moodle@main`; the OASIS DITA 1.2 and 1.3 specifications, WCAG 2.2 Understanding documents and the ARIA Authoring Practices carousel pattern were read at w3.org and docs.oasis-open.org; Mayer's 2014 *Multimedia Instruction* chapter was extracted from PDF and each effect size grepped in context. Where a source was unreachable — Docebo, Absorb, TalentLMS and Litmos article bodies were all behind Cloudflare, 403s or login walls, and archive.org was offline — the claim was demoted rather than repeated. **Every one of those demotions is preserved below under a "Not verified" heading. A claim landing there means we could not reach the source, not that it is false.** Vendor-published statistics are labelled as vendor-published every time they appear, without exception, because in this category the vendor almost always both defines the metric and profits from the definition.

**Read the state of the code before reading the recommendations.** Several structural critiques in the source reports were written against `~/.Trash/relayhq-abandoned-2026-08-16`, the abandoned Bun/Elysia/SQLite predecessor, not against the live prototype. The live repo has already made the single most important change: `src/store/schema.js` lifts `knowledge` and `subforms` to top-level collections and has catalog items reference them by id (`item.knowledgeIds`, `item.subformIds`), which is epic W2.2 in `plan.md`. It also already declares `contacts`, `organizations`, `jobFunctions`, `slaPolicies`, `curricula`, `courses` and `enrollments` as state keys, and ships **13** form field types including the two relational ones (`user`, `asset`) — not the 8 the older brief describes. So the "author once" thesis is structurally *possible* in the current code. It is not yet *demonstrated*: `src/store/seed/knowledge.js` and `src/store/seed/learning.js` are both empty placeholders, and `Knowledge.jsx` and `Learning.jsx` are both `Construction` stubs. This document is the specification for filling them.

## 1. The tri-modal thesis, tested

**Short answer: the thesis holds, but not in the shape it is usually pitched. Two of the three legs are table stakes, one is genuinely open ground, and the seam between them will break in a specific, nameable way that must be designed around rather than talked around.**

### 1.1 Legs (a) and (b) are already shipped by other people

"One knowledge corpus serving both a customer-facing help surface and an agent-facing surface" is not a differentiator. It is a shipped feature at multiple vendors, verified:

- **Front** ships `KnowledgeBaseResponse.type: internal | external` — one enum on one corpus, two audiences.
- **Zendesk** does it at article granularity: `Article.user_segment_id`, "The id of the user segment which defines who can see this article. Set to null to make it accessible to everyone", paired with `permission_group_id` for edit rights.
- **Zoomin** delivers one content library into a documentation portal, in-product help, a ServiceNow agent console and Salesforce. **It is now "Zoomin from Salesforce" — the acquisition completed 1 November 2024** and the capability is being folded into Data Cloud and Agentforce.
- **Stonly** ships **Reusable Snippets** into a public knowledge base, an embeddable widget, in-app embeds, and agent views inside Zendesk, Salesforce, Freshdesk and ServiceNow. Positioning, verbatim: "Create and distribute knowledge that makes it easier for customers, support reps and AI to quickly resolve cases."
- **Heretto** sells a DITA CCMS plus a Deploy API and a customer-facing Portal with SSO and role-based personalisation.

If RelayHQ's pitch stops at (a)+(b), the honest competitive answer is "Stonly already does that, and Salesforce bought the enterprise version of it." Do not build the deck around deflection-plus-enablement.

### 1.2 Leg (c) is the open ground, and the architecture for it is a ratified standard

Nobody found in this research composes the *same* content objects into an assessed, certifiable curriculum. The evidence for that gap is unusually good because it comes from the vendor best positioned to close it:

> **Gainsight owns both sides and still describes the link as federation.** Gainsight acquired Northpass (11 July 2023) and separately acquired Skilljar (2 April 2025) — two distinct acquisitions, not one asset rebranded. It therefore owns an academy product and a community/knowledge product. Its own Customer Communities page describes the integration as Community SSO plus federated search: users can "navigate directly to Skilljar courses, find Academy content in the community search, and filter results between community posts and Skilljar courses." It calls this "uniquely deep, native" — and it is still two platforms sharing a search box.

Corroborating absences, all from primary vendor pages: Thought Industries organises around Create / Deliver / Monetize / Measure / Integrate and uses no single-sourcing or knowledge-base-integration language at all. Intellum's own "education platform vs CMS" argument is about active-vs-passive engagement and a native report writer, never about content objects. WorkRamp's glossary defines reuse as an object appearing in multiple Paths and describes no mechanism spanning a help centre and a course. Document360 ships a Support Ticket Deflector, decision trees, a widget, an internal KB and AI search — and no course, module, lesson, assessment or completion layer. HubSpot runs `academy.hubspot.com` and `knowledge.hubspot.com` as separate platforms with cross-links.

The architecture RelayHQ wants is not speculative. **OASIS DITA 1.2's Learning & Training specialization (OASIS Standard, 2010) is the thesis, ratified fifteen years ago.** It keeps concept/task/reference topics as the atoms and uses a learning map domain — `learningGroup` > `learningObject` > topicrefs — to assemble those same atoms into courses. `learningGroup` is verbatim "A map container and optional topic reference to introduce and group learning objects into higher-level organizations, such as course-level, module-level, or lesson-level." `learningContent` "Provides the learning content itself and **enables direct use of content from DITA task, concept, and reference topics**." Stated design objective #5, verbatim: "Build on existing DITA infrastructures as much as possible… with minimal adaptation they can use standard approaches for DITA content and **reuse content previously developed for other purposes**."

So the correct positioning claim is narrow and defensible: *the content-delivery vendors demonstrably ship deflection plus agent enablement and demonstrably do not ship a curriculum layer over the same atoms; the LMS vendors reuse atoms within training and have no help surface; the architecture that unifies them is a public standard nobody has productised on one substrate.* The broad claim "nobody does all three" is **likely, not verified** — the LMS/employee-enablement side (Docebo, 360Learning, Guru, Notion) was not swept, and Salesforce now owns Zoomin, Agentforce and Trailhead under one roof. Re-check before saying it to an investor.

### 1.3 Where it genuinely breaks — and this is the part not to soften

The standard states the failure mode in its own words, and it is the whole risk in one sentence:

> "Simply using the content models described in this specification, of course, does not ensure quality learning content. Quality learning content only results from good instructional design and in-depth learning needs analysis."

The architecture buys **distribution** of content across three surfaces for free. It buys **no pedagogy**. If the pitch is "our knowledge base automatically becomes a course," it will be tested by the first buyer with an L&D function and it will fail. Five specific breakages, each with an evidenced cause:

1. **Voice / isolation.** DITA's definition of a topic is the acceptance criterion: "a titled unit of information that can be understood in isolation and used in multiple contexts… short enough to address a single subject or answer a single question but long enough to make sense on its own." A support atom must stand alone. A lesson is *deliberately dependent* on what came before it. A support article is also written reactively, for a reader who already has the problem and knows what to search for; a lesson addresses someone who does not yet know what to ask. Those are different registers, and the mismatch is real.
2. **Motivation.** A course needs "why this matters" and a stated objective. Putting that into the article body bloats the deflection surface, where the reader wants the answer in one screen and nothing else.
3. **Granularity.** A good support atom answers one reader question. A good lesson is usually several tasks plus a concept. The relationship is many-to-one, never 1:1 — which means a Lesson cannot simply *be* an atom pointer with no structure of its own.
4. **Assessment cannot be derived.** DITA keeps Learning Assessment as a separate topic type, and `learningObject` references overview / content / summary / assessment as distinct roles, precisely because you cannot infer a question from a procedure without authoring intent.
5. **Sequence.** A course may assume prior lessons; an article may assume nothing.

**The honest mitigation, and it is cheap:** the atom never changes, and the pedagogy lives on the *placement*, not the content. A Lesson is `{ knowledgeId, role, framing }` and holds **no body field at all**. The atom keeps its reference voice; a small authored framing block on the Lesson — intro, why-it-matters, check-yourself, skip-if-known — supplies the teaching voice. This is exactly DITA's map/topic split, and it is what "author once" actually means. The defensible pitch is therefore not "the article *is* the lesson" but **"the article is the lesson's body, and the wrapper is thin."** That claim survives a skeptic; the other one does not.

Two structural guard rails make the mitigation stick. First, a publish gate: a Lesson cannot publish if its atom lacks `learning.objective` and `learning.estimatedMinutes`. Second, author-time lint on the isolation rule — an atom whose body matches `/as (described|shown) (above|below)|in the previous step|see above/i` fails, because that phrase is the exact thing that breaks when the atom moves surfaces.

### 1.4 The measurement problem, stated plainly

**There is no disinterested third-party statistic anywhere in this research on how much self-service content actually deflects.** That is not an oversight; it was searched for specifically across four passes and none was found. What exists:

- **Zendesk's own published formula is a population ratio, not a count**: "Ticket deflection rate = Total users of your help center(s) ÷ Total users in tickets." It observes no counterfactual — a ticket never filed leaves no record. Deflection is definitionally a proxy, and Zendesk's article cites no third-party research.
- **Vendor case studies span 5% to 80%**, all self-reported and uncontrolled: Zenefits 5% (Skilljar case study, with 55% course completion and only 13% of new users starting training at all); Absorb 15% (self-published, method: their L&D team analysed their own Zendesk, took the top ten recurring issues, built micro-courses in two-week sprints — no period, no comparison group); Alarm.com 17% ("partners that had taken training over a certain period of time versus partners that hadn't"); Handshake "50% faster TTV for trained customers"; **Jamf 80%, whose linked Skilljar case study renders "No items found" in all three of its Challenge / Solution / Results sections.**
- **The best-designed of these are trained-vs-untrained cohort comparisons, which cannot support a causal claim.** Customers who complete training are more engaged, better resourced, further along the lifecycle and likelier to have a dedicated admin — each of which independently predicts fewer how-to tickets. "Trained customers file 17% fewer tickets" is true and useful; "training caused a 17% reduction" does not follow.
- **The structural argument rests on an uncited number.** Skilljar states verbatim that "Research shows that 48% of support inquiries at technology companies are 'how-to' in nature," with no link, reference or attribution, and it is the only statistic on that page. If roughly half of support volume is how-to, the deflection thesis is structurally sound without a causal study — which makes tracing that figure worth doing before it appears in any RelayHQ material.
- **Every AI vendor has repriced to per-outcome and none publishes an audited rate.** Zendesk bills automated resolutions above a plan allowance; Intercom Fin "$0.99 per outcome"; Help Scout AI Answers "$0.75/resolution"; Front Autopilot "Starting at $0.05 /conversation"; Gorgias "pay only when it resolves a conversation". All five are vendor pricing pages. Treat the *pricing model* as verified and any implied *efficacy* as unverified.
- **KCS's headline benefits are explicitly anecdotal by the Consortium's own framing** — "Improved resolution times by 25-50%" is presented as the experience of members who have been on the KCS journey a few years, with no method, sample or citation. Treat 25–50% as a practitioner rule of thumb. The Consortium sells KCS certification, training and exams, so it is an interested party even though it is a nonprofit.

**Consequence for the product, not just the deck:** RelayHQ cannot borrow a credible deflection number from the market and must generate its own. That is a feature, not a weakness — RelayHQ owns both the drill path and the form, so it can log the actual sequence of atoms a requester saw before submitting or abandoning, which no two-system stack can. Label the metric **"assisted resolution"**, never "tickets deflected", and print the definition under the tile alongside an explicit disclaimer that no system can observe a request that was never made. Being the only vendor in the category that says that out loud is worth more than a borrowed 40%.

Likewise: **no verified figure exists anywhere in this research for adoption lift, course-completion benchmarks, or training ROI.** Do not let one enter the deck by inference.

## 2. Internal ITSM vs external customer service — what actually differs

The differences are not in workflow. They are in **identity, entry point and accountability**. Freshworks' own SKU split is the cleanest evidence: Freshservice's product page positions ITSM for enterprise IT — incident, on-call, change, asset, CMDB, service catalog, dependency mapping, employee onboarding — and **never mentions Freshdesk or customer support anywhere on the page**. Freshdesk's ticket object is unmistakably external (`requester_id`, `company_id`, `product_id`, `source`, `fr_due_by`, `cc_emails`, `spam`). The vendor split the product by *requester*, not by feature set.

| Dimension | Internal ITSM | External customer service | What RelayHQ needs to support both |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Requester identity** | A directory record. One authoritative identity in Entra/LDAP, authenticated by SSO. | A *claim attached to a handle*. Front models `ContactHandle {handle, source}` with source ∈ `twitter, email, phone, facebook, intercom, front_chat, custom`. Gorgias ships `Customer.channels` plus a `merge-customers` endpoint whose docs describe merging source into target and deleting it, erroring on integration conflicts. | `Contact` with a `handles[]` array of `{source, value, verified}`, plus a `mergeContacts(sourceId, targetId)` reducer that unions handles, reassigns conversations, and **surfaces a conflict rather than silently overwriting** when both hold data for the same integration key. |
| **Organization** | Group membership, assigned. | *Inferred from email domain.* Zendesk `Organization.domain_names`; Front `AccountResponse.domains` + `external_id`. Zendesk adds a B2B visibility control with no internal analogue: `shared_tickets` ("End users in this organization are able to see each other's tickets") and `shared_comments`. Pure-B2C drops it entirely — Gorgias's 22-object API contains **zero** case-insensitive occurrences of "company" or "organization". | `Organization` **optional**, with `domains[]`, `sharedTickets`, `tier`. Resolve org on conversation create by matching the contact's email domain — a pure function. One unified queue, never per-channel tabs — the unification is the point. Channel glyph in a fixed left gutter column; hue used only on the glyph, never on row backgrounds. |

## 3. The training layer

### 3.1 The hierarchy, and why this vocabulary

Every product examined lands on **four container levels and no more**, and the leaf is always a single piece of content with a completion rule attached. Only the naming diverges:

| Product | L1 | L2 | L3 | L4 (leaf) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WorkRamp** | Path | Guide | Section | Task | verbatim verified |
| **Rise 360** | — | Course | Section | Lesson (→ Blocks) | verified |
| **Moodle** | — | Course | Section | Course module | verified |
| **Absorb** | Curriculum | Course | Chapter | Learning Object | Learning Objects article verified; the rest not |
| **Docebo** | Learning plan | Course | Folder | Training material | learning-plans article verified; the rest not |
| **DITA L&T** | `learningGroup` (course-level) | `learningGroup` (module-level) | `learningGroup` (lesson-level) | `learningObject` → topicrefs | verbatim verified |

**Adopt: Curriculum → Course → Module → Lesson.**

- **"Curriculum" over "Learning Path"** — RelayHQ's differentiator is job-function *completeness*. *Path* implies a route; *Curriculum* implies coverage.
- **"Module" over "Section"** (overloaded by form layouts in this product) and over **"Chapter"** (reads book-like).
- **"Guide" is unavailable as a lesson-format name.** WorkRamp uses Guide for a whole container and Litmos uses the word the same way. The live schema declares `format: 'article'|'guide'`; the product framing calls the second form **slides**. **Rename the schema value `'guide'` → `'slides'`** — it names the shape rather than an intent, and it removes a collision with two competitors' container vocabulary.
- **Modules are embedded** inside a Course. No product examined reuses a chapter across courses; do not build for it.
- **Lessons are references.** `Lesson` holds `knowledgeId` and carries **no content field**. This is the single load-bearing decision in the module. A copy makes the training layer a fork of the knowledge base, and help content and course content will have visibly diverged within a quarter. Both the CCMS world (Heretto's `conref` pulls an element by ID from a warehouse topic so one edit propagates) and the commercial world (Stonly's Reusable Snippets) do it by reference, and the documented failure mode of copying is exactly the drift RelayHQ is claiming to solve.

**Precedent that cross-container shared credit is a real, wanted feature:** WorkRamp ships an article titled **"Synced Guide Progress Across Paths"** describing how to "setup a Guide to be automatically marked as completed across multiple Paths." Note the detail — it lives in WorkRamp's **Guides** collection and is an **opt-in, per-guide configuration**, not automatic platform behaviour. That opt-in framing is the better precedent to copy: even the vendor that shipped shared credit treats it as a choice per atom. Encode it as `Course.creditPolicy: 'shared' | 'perCourse'`, defaulting to `'shared'`.

### 3.2 The mechanic no competitor can copy: record completion against the atom

**Completion rules belong on the placement. The completion record belongs to the content.** That split is a design judgment, not a vendor finding, and it should be labelled as such — but it is what makes deflection credit toward training, and no competitor's help centre and LMS share a record store, so no competitor can copy it.

```
LessonRecord = { id, userId, knowledgeId,
  status: 'not_started'|'in_progress'|'completed',
  firstViewedAt, completedAt?, secondsSpent, viewedSlideIds?: string[],
  contentVersionAtCompletion?: number,
  source: 'course'|'deflection'|'agent_context'|'manual_admin',
  sourceRef?: string,        // ticketId or catalog itemId
  attempts: Attempt[] }
```

An agent who opened the runbook while working REQ-4471 has demonstrably consumed the content, so that lesson is already green when onboarding is assigned. Render it green **with a provenance chip** — "Completed 12 Mar · read while working REQ-4471" — and a "Review anyway" link. Never silently skip it; a learner who cannot see why a lesson is green will not trust the score.

Course progress is **derived, never stored**: `(# required LessonRefs whose knowledgeId has a satisfying record) / (# required LessonRefs)`. **Count each required lesson as exactly one unit. Never weight by minutes or credits, and never mix units in one denominator** — any weighted scheme summing heterogeneous requirement types produces numbers a learner cannot predict, and an unpredictable progress bar is worse than none. *(The source draft justified this with an Absorb worked example producing 2.91%; that article could not be verified and the rule stands on first principles instead. See §4.2 Not verified.)*

**Guard rail, verified from Absorb's own words:** "When a Learning Object is deleted, such as an Observation Checklist, data associated with that Learning Object in LMS reporting is also deleted." LessonRecords are keyed by `knowledgeId` and must survive removal from every course. Deleting an atom must warn with the count of affected records and offer archive rather than delete.

### 3.3 What a KB atom needs added to work as a lesson

Keep the envelope **optional at atom level and required only when a published Lesson references the atom**. That keeps authoring cost off the support team until someone builds a course, and it means a two-sentence help snippet cannot silently become a lesson.

| Field | Purpose | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `objective` | What the learner can do afterwards. Renders as a lead-in card in the course surface, suppressed in the support surface. | Present in the live schema. **Do not attach an effect size to it.** Mayer's expectation-principle d = 0.83 is for *sample pre-questions*, and he writes explicitly: "there is promising initial support for the expectation principle, but more research is needed, including additional research on providing students with a statement of the instructional objective." |
| `preQuestion` *(new, optional)* | The actually-measured intervention — a sample test item shown before the lesson. | Mayer, Dow & Mayer (2003), d = 0.83. If you want the measured effect, render this, not a mission statement. |
| `keyTerms: {term, definition}[]` *(new)* | A pretraining screen the learner taps through before step 1; collapses into an inline glossary popover on the support surface. **Same data, different render — this is the thesis working.** | Mayer's **pretraining principle**: "people learn better from a complex lesson when they receive pretraining in the names and characteristics of the key concepts", median **d = 0.85** across five experiments and **d = 0.88** across ten, described as "strong and consistent evidence". Roughly double Hattie's d > 0.40 practical-relevance bar. |
| `minutes` | Makes a curriculum plannable and is what learners actually look at. Course and curriculum durations **derive by summing atoms**, so editing one article's estimate visibly moves the curriculum total. | Duration labels are the most consistent convention across real academies — Asana labels every card ("20 min", "60 min", "3 hr"), HubSpot exposes Duration as a top-level catalog facet. |
| `skillIds` *(new)* | The join between an atom and a job function. | Litmos ships **Competencies** and **Equivalencies** as named features (slugs verified; behaviour not). |
| `contentVersion`, `lastMaterialChangeAt` *(new)* | Bumped only on substantive body/slide edits, never on tag or title edits. Powers the drift signal. | See §3.5. |
| `check` + `checkConfig` | `{ passScore (default 80), attempts (null = unlimited), shuffleQuestions, shuffleOptions, revealAnswers }`. | Rise's nine verified quiz settings, minus the cosmetic ones. Rise's defaults verified verbatim: "The default is 80 percent"; "By default, learners can attempt a quiz an unlimited number of times." |
| `informationType` *(new axis)* | `concept \| task \| reference \| policy \| troubleshooting` — **orthogonal to `format`**. `format` is a presentation axis and cannot answer the question a course composer needs answered: can this atom serve as the overview, the procedure or the reference for this lesson? | DITA information typing: "Topics that answer different reader questions (How …? What is …?) can be categorized with different information types", and authors should "Avoid mixing content types, thereby losing reader focus." It also makes the deflection surface smarter — the Item page can order concept → task → reference by computation rather than by hand-sorting. |
| **`prerequisiteIds` — MOVE OFF the atom** | Delete from `KnowledgeItem`; put it on `LessonRef`. A prerequisite is a property of a **position in a sequence**, not of an article. The same runbook is a prerequisite for nothing when it is a help topic. | Design judgment, following the map/topic split. |
| `lessonReady` *(derived, not stored)* | `status === 'published' && !!objective && minutes > 0 && (format === 'article' ? body.length > MIN : slides.length >= 2)`. **Question types:** extend the schema's `'single'|'multi'|'boolean'` with `'ordering'` and `'match'`. Five covers Rise's verified four (Multiple Choice; Multiple Response; Fill in the Blank, "Up to 10 acceptable answers per question"; Matching, "Add up to 10 text-based matching pairs per question", left column shuffled, right fixed) and TalentLMS's core set. Skip free-text (needs grading) and hot-spot (needs an image editor) — noting that WorkRamp ships **eleven** evidence-of-performance types including both, plus Video Recording, Voice Recording and a 21 CFR Part 11–compliant E-Signature. WorkRamp invests in proving a learner can *do* the job; pure authoring tools do not. That is the natural v2.

### 3.4 Gating, in three tiers, permissive by default

Keep the enums small, evaluate them as a **pure function** over `(course, curriculum, lessonRecords, userId)` computed on render, and allow no cross-object side effects. No stored lock state means no lock state to go stale — the client-side advantage, not a limitation.

- **Course:** `navigation: 'free' | 'sequential'`. Default `'free'`. *(Rise: "With Restrict Navigation toggled to On, learners have to take lessons in order" — and it also disables search and manual completion.)*
- **Module:** `unlock: 'always' | 'afterPreviousModule'`. Default `'always'`.
- **Lesson:** `gate: 'view' | 'viewAllSlides' | 'minTime' | 'check' | 'manual'`.
  - `view` — Moodle `completionview_desc`: "View the activity"
  - `viewAllSlides` — RelayHQ-specific; the natural rule for the slides format
  - `minTime` — TalentLMS: "**After a period of time:** Users can click the completion button only after the specified number of seconds has passed"
  - `check` — TalentLMS: "**With a question:** Users must correctly answer the selected question before completing the unit" / Rise: "Require Passing Score to Continue". *(TalentLMS's reporting trap, verbatim: "**Note: Answers to completion questions are not tracked in reports.**" If you want the data, it must be a real assessment, not a gate question.)*
  - `manual` — Moodle: "Students must manually mark the activity as done"
- **Curriculum:** `Track.paceProgress` forces completion of each track before the next unlocks. *(Field name borrowed from unverified Absorb documentation — keep the field, do not attribute the name.)*

The best in-lesson mechanic to copy is **Rise's Continue block**, verified verbatim with exactly three Completion Types: "**None** means learners simply need to click the button to continue"; "**Complete Block Directly Above**"; "**Complete All Blocks Above**". Free navigation plus targeted Continue-block gates is the right default for RelayHQ's audience — adults trying to get back to work. **Present that combination as RelayHQ's design judgment, not as Articulate's recommendation**; their navigation article describes the two features separately and never recommends combining them.

Express every gate as a **sticky footer bar stating the condition in plain language with live progress** — "Continue — 3 of 8 slides", "Continue in 0:42", "Answer 2 questions to continue" — not as a disabled sidebar. Locked modules name their specific blocker in a tooltip ("Unlocks after Module 2: Escalation paths"). An unexplained lock is a support ticket in a product about support tickets.

### 3.5 Content drift — the risk single-sourcing creates, turned into a feature

In a single-source architecture this problem gets **worse**, not better: an author fixing a help article on a Tuesday afternoon has silently changed a compliance course. Three vendors have three different partial answers and none is good — Docebo forces an admin through a three-way recalculation choice (no recalculation / recalculate all completed enrollments / recalculate for specific branches and groups, with a worked example where "100%" is "recalculated and set to 50%" after two courses are added); Litmos maintains an article literally titled **"Completion and Recalculation Behavior"**; Absorb deletes reporting data with the Learning Object.

`KnowledgeItem.contentVersion` + `lastMaterialChangeAt`; `LessonRecord.contentVersionAtCompletion`. Derived, nothing stored: `staleLessons` = required refs where `contentVersion > contentVersionAtCompletion`. Optional `Course.onContentChange: 'ignore'|'flag'|'requireReview'`, where `requireReview` moves affected enrollments back to `in_progress` **for that lesson only** — the surgical version of a blunt recalculate-all.

On the Knowledge editor, before saving a substantive edit to an atom used in published courses, show an **inline notice, not a blocking modal**: "This article is a lesson in 2 courses. 47 people have completed it." **Inform, do not obstruct.** Obstruction trains authors to stop fixing help content, which is the worst possible outcome for the deflection surface.

### 3.6 Standards: adopt the vocabulary, implement none of the runtimes

A client-side prototype with no backend cannot host a SCORM run-time adapter or an LRS and should not try. But the standards have already solved the naming problem RelayHQ is about to solve badly by inventing its own status words, and all of these are verified against primary sources:

- **cmi5 `moveOn`, verbatim, exactly five values**: `Passed, Completed, CompletedAndPassed, CompletedOrPassed, NotApplicable` (NotApplicable is the spec default). Adopt on `LessonRef`.
- **SCORM 2004 split completion from success** — `cmi.completion_status` (`completed, incomplete, not attempted, unknown`) is a different field from `cmi.success_status` (`passed, failed, unknown`). Keep them separate: `status` answers "did they get through it", `Attempt.passed` answers "did they meet the bar". A learner can be completed-and-failed, which is a real and reportable state, and conflating them is the mistake RelayHQ would otherwise make.
- **xAPI** is actor-verb-object statements posted to a Learning Record Store which "can exist on its own or inside an LMS", and is "an IEEE approved standard (IEEE 9274.1.1-2023)". Shape `LessonRecord` so `toXapiStatement(record, user, atom)` is ~30 lines of serialisation, not a rewrite.
- **schema.org** already defines the metadata vocabulary, verified field by field: `LearningResource` has `teaches`, `assesses`, `competencyRequired`, `educationalLevel`, `educationalUse`, `learningResourceType`, `educationalAlignment`; `HowTo` has `step`, `totalTime`, `performTime`, `prepTime`, `supply`, `tool`, `yield`, `estimatedCost`. Name RelayHQ's fields to match and JSON-LD serialisation is a pure function with no mapping table. *(Temper one expectation: Google retired HowTo rich results in general web search in 2023. Sell this as portability and AI retrieval, not SERP treatment.)*

If a prospect asks about SCORM, the honest and stronger answer is that RelayHQ authors content **natively** rather than importing black boxes — which is precisely why the same atom can serve deflection and enablement. A SCORM package can serve neither.

### 3.7 The differentiating move: generate the curriculum from the catalog

This is where RelayHQ stops copying and starts leading, and it is upstream of everything else. RelayHQ knows what a Tier-1 Support Agent is *responsible for*, because the catalog is a Product → Subcategory → Item tree and Items already reference knowledge by id. So "everything a Tier-1 Support Agent must know" can be **computed** as the set of atoms reachable from the catalog subtrees that job function owns. `jobFunctions` is already a top-level state key.

**No surveyed product can do this, because none of them owns a service catalog** — they all make a human type the course list by hand. *(State this as a limitation of the products whose documentation we could read. Litmos and Seismic docs are login-gated and were never read; Docebo and Absorb were unreachable. The design opportunity is unaffected; the market-wide absolute is not established.)*

Two screens follow. A **curriculum builder** with ordered tracks and a requirement chip per track ("all 4" / "any 2 of 5" / "optional"). And the screen that sells the product: a **coverage matrix**, rows = catalog Items under the job function's subtrees, columns = has a knowledge atom? / atom is `lessonReady`? / atom appears in a course in this curriculum? Red cells are literal gaps in what the role is being taught, traced to the catalog. A "Generate starter curriculum" button walks the subtrees and proposes one Course per Product, one Module per Subcategory, lessons pre-populated from each Item's `knowledgeIds`; the author deletes down. **Building a course by deletion is far faster than building one by search, and it is only possible because RelayHQ owns the catalog.**

### 4.1 External customer service platforms

**Verified findings.**

1. **Identity is a merged set of channel handles, not a user record.** Front `ContactHandle {handle, source}`; source enum exactly `twitter, email, phone, facebook, intercom, front_chat, custom`. Gorgias's merge endpoint, verbatim: "Merge two customers according to the data sent in the payload. The data of the source customer will be merged into the target customer and then deleted. In case both customers have data for the same integration a conflict will occur with the API returning an error." Identity resolution and merge are first-class product surfaces externally and never needed internally.
2. **Organization membership is inferred from email domain** — Zendesk `Organization.domain_names`, Front `AccountResponse.domains` + `external_id`; plus the B2B controls `shared_tickets` and `shared_comments`.
3. **B2C drops the organization entirely.** Gorgias's `llms.txt` documents exactly **22** objects (Account, Customer, CustomField, Event, File, Integration, Job, Macro, Rule, SatisfactionSurvey, Statistic, Tag, Team, Ticket, TicketHttpIntegration, TicketMessage, User, View, VoiceCall, VoiceCallEvent, VoiceCallRecording, Widget) with **zero** case-insensitive matches for "company" or "organization" (verified by direct grep). Its `Account` is the Gorgias tenant. The replacement is `Customer.integrations`, verbatim "Data coming from integrations (Shopify, Recharge, HTTP, etc...)… Keys of the object are integration IDs, values are raw data collected from the integrations" — schemaless by design and trivially implementable client-side.
4. **Per-field, per-audience visibility is a convergent pattern.** See §2. Three independent vendors, same split.
5. **Customer-facing states need two names plus a share flag.** Intercom `ticket_state_internal_label` / `ticket_state_external_label` / `is_shared`.
6. **"Waiting on customer" is universal and every vendor denormalizes a waiting timestamp.** Intercom `waiting_since`, Front `waiting_since` ("Timestamp of the oldest unreplied message"), Help Scout `customerWaitingSince`.
7. **Conversation-first vs ticket-first is a visible seam.** Front bolts ticket statuses onto a conversation core; Front also types conversations `conversation|discussion|task`, where discussion is internal-only.
8. **Internal notes vs public replies, three structural approaches.** Flag on the message (Gorgias, Zendesk) — cheapest and the majority; enum on the reply payload (Intercom `message_type: comment|note|quick_reply`); separate entity (Front `CommentResponse`, with `is_pinned`). Take the flag.
9. **The SLA metric vocabulary is small and shared.** Zendesk defines exactly six ticket SLA metrics — first reply time, next reply time, periodic update time, requester wait time, agent work time, total resolution time — plus, verbatim, "There is only one measurement for a group SLA service target: group ownership time." Front's `AnalyticsMetricId` enum (25 values) independently covers the same ground plus CSAT metrics. A prototype needs two to be credible.
10. **SLA policies are an ordered first-match list**, with targets per priority. **Correction carried through from fact-checking:** it is *not* true that conditions cannot reference ticket status — Zendesk states that with custom ticket statuses activated, you can condition a policy on status. Only priority is genuinely excluded, because it is the axis along which targets already vary.
11. **Macros are action bundles, not canned text — this is widely misunderstood.** Gorgias, verbatim: "A macro (canned response) is a list of actions that can be applied to tickets to modify them, and/or reply to them… a single macro can add a tag to the ticket, assign an agent to it and send a pre-defined response." It carries `actions`, `usage` ("How many times the macro was applied on a ticket"), `intent` and `language`. Front's `MessageTemplateResponse` is text-only but scoped by `inbox_ids`. **For RelayHQ, add one macro action nobody else has: `attach_kb`, which inserts a KB atom into the reply — the fourth reuse of the same content atom, at almost no cost.**
12. **Article helpfulness voting is the standard deflection primitive.** Zendesk Article: `vote_count` ("The total number of upvotes and downvotes") and `vote_sum` ("The sum of upvotes (+1) and downvotes (-1)"); identical pair on Gather Posts. Content is scored by the people it was supposed to deflect.
13. **Help-center content trees are ~3 levels with content as leaves.** Zendesk Guide: Category → Section → Article, where `Section.parent_section_id` is "Only writable for Guide Enterprise customers" — deeper nesting is deliberately an enterprise escape hatch. **Intercom has no standalone Section object**; the second level is a nested Collection via a self-referential `collection.parent_id` ("If `null` then it is the first level collection"). RelayHQ's Product → Subcategory → Item is a well-precedented shape. Two refinements: RelayHQ's leaf is a request-catalog Item carrying references rather than a content article, and RelayHQ's tree is **2–3 levels, not strictly 3** — `CatalogNode.children` permits Items to attach directly to a Product.
14. **Structured in-conversation replies are a message type, not a separate wizard surface.** Intercom `quick_reply` with `reply_options`, whose items are **objects** (`{text, uuid}`), not strings. This is the natural way to push a slides walkthrough into a live thread and let the customer answer without free text.
15. **Email threading requires quote-stripping** — an external-only chore that is routinely underestimated. Gorgias stores `body_text`, `body_html`, `stripped_text` ("The text version of the body of the message without email signatures and previous replies"), `stripped_html`, `stripped_signature`, and keeps `message_id` (the ID on the sending service) separate from its own `id` and `external_id`.
16. **Per-conversation statistics are denormalized, not computed at read time.** Intercom's `conversation_statistics` carries exactly **18** precomputed fields; several are documented as subtracting out-of-business-hours. For a client-side prototype this is encouraging — they are cheap derived values over a message array, and precomputing on write keeps list views fast with no backend.
17. **Custom-field type systems run 7–11 types.** Zendesk 11 (text, textarea, checkbox, date, integer, decimal, regexp, partialcreditcard, multiselect, tagger, lookup); Front 7; Intercom 7. Every vendor except Intercom includes a **relational** type — Zendesk's `lookup` with `relationship_target_type` (`zen:user`, `zen:organization`, `zen:ticket`, `zen:custom_object:{key}`), Front's `teammate` and `inbox`. **The live RelayHQ schema already ships 13 types including relational `user` and `asset`**, so this gap is closed; what is missing is a `lookup` targeting the new `contact` / `organization` entities once they carry data.

### 4.4 Single-source architecture and the slides viewer

**Verified findings.**

1. **DITA implements the thesis and states its own failure mode** — §1.2 and §1.3.
2. **DITA's topic definition is a usable acceptance criterion** — §1.3. This is the lint rule.
3. **The DITA taskbody maps cleanly onto a slide deck.** Strict order verified: `<prereq>` > `<context>` > `<steps>` > `<result>` > `<example>` > `<postreq>`, and "Each step in a task must contain a command `<cmd>` element which describes the particular action the user must perform." Map it: `deck.prereqNote` = prereq, `deck.goal` = context, `slide` = step, `slide.caption` = **cmd (required, imperative, one action)**, `slide.detail` = info, `slide.expected` = stepresult, `deck.outcome` = result. That gives required-field validation for free and makes the same deck renderable as prose without re-authoring. **Version correction: `<steptroubleshooting>` is DITA 1.3, not 1.2** — the 1.2 optional step children are `<info>`, `<substeps>`, `<tutorialinfo>`, `<stepxmp>`, `<choices>`, `<stepresult>`.
4. **Reuse breaks structurally, and DITA says how.** Verbatim from the 1.3 conref processing spec: "a conref processor MUST NOT permit resolution of a reuse relationship that could be rendered invalid under the rules of either the reused or reusing content," and validity applies "not simply to the current content at the time of replacement, but to the possible content given the restrictions of both the referencing document type and the referenced document type." **Practical consequence:** a client-side reuse model must decide up front whether a reference resolves against the atom's own context or the containing surface's context. Cross-references inside an atom ("see the approval policy") are the exact thing that breaks when the atom appears in a course.
5. **Paligo ships eight kinds of reuse and warns about it in its own docs** — topic, publication, structure, text fragment, informal topic, admonition, variables, filtering/profiling; "Every paragraph that you create is stored in a database and is available for reuse"; and, verbatim, "If you are new to content reuse, structured authoring and single-sourcing, it's easy to be overwhelmed by the reuse features." **Take the narrow end.**
6. **Docs-to-training is a shipped commercial pipeline, and the incumbent answer is export.** MadCap Flare, verbatim: "Deliver responsive outputs to HTML5, PDF, Word, PowerPoint®, SCORM, and more"; "Topic-based authoring turns content into reusable blocks for guides, training, help sites, and more"; publishes directly to Salesforce, Zendesk and ServiceNow. **RelayHQ's differentiator is rendering the course on the same substrate rather than exporting it to an LMS.**
7. **Mayer's segmenting principle validates a LEARNER-PACED tap-to-advance viewer** — and this is the single most on-point result in the research. Verbatim: "The segmenting principle is that people learn better when a complex lesson is presented in manageable parts." The canonical experiment compared a continuous 2.5-minute narrated animation against one that "paused after each of 16 segments **until the learner clicked a 'Continue' button**". Median **d = 0.98** across three experiments and **d = 0.82** across nine. Effects "may be strongest for low-knowledge learners" (Ayres, 2006). The practical-relevance bar Mayer uses, verbatim: "principles that consistently generate effect sizes greater than d = 0.40, which Hattie (2009) argues is the level needed for practical relevance." **The mechanic RelayHQ wants is one of the better-evidenced things in instructional design. The auto-advancing variant is the part with no supporting evidence.**
8. **Two Mayer principles weaken or vanish under learner-controlled pacing.** Temporal contiguity: median **d = 1.31** across eight experiments, Ginns (2006) **d = 0.87** across 13 — but "The effects may be diminished when learners have control over the pace and order of presentation." Modality: median **d = 1.02** across 17 experiments and **d = 0.88** across 35, "more widely tested than any other principle" — but "the modality effect was not obtained in a study in which the pace of the lesson was slow and under learner control," and its listed boundary condition is "lesson is fast-paced and under system control." **Consequence: a self-paced, silent, captioned slide is the design where the audio advantage does not apply. Shipping without narration gives up nothing and dodges the redundancy penalty (d = 0.72) at the same time.**
9. **Spatial contiguity and coherence give checkable layout rules.** Spatial contiguity: words "placed next to the portion of the diagram they described rather than as a caption at the bottom", median **d = 1.12**; Ginns (2006) meta-analysis of 37 experiments **d = 0.71**, and **d = 1.07** for published transfer measures. Coherence: **d = 1.66**, the largest effect in Mayer's set, with seductive details costing d = 0.88 and background music d = 1.11. **Concrete rules: anchor the caption to a `hotspot` rect on the screenshot rather than a bar below it; crop aggressively and strip browser chrome; no decorative imagery, no background music, no ambient motion.**
10. **Signaling and on-screen presenters are weaker than assumed.** Verbal signaling **d = 0.52** ("moderate evidence"); visual signaling with arrows "was not found to be effective in promoting transfer posttest performance" in two studies. Adding an instructor's image yields **d = 0.26** — below the bar. With no narration the **multimedia principle** applies instead: **d = 1.39** across 11 experiments for words+pictures over words alone. **Invest in the imperative caption and the crop, not in animated arrows or an avatar.** And, verbatim: "providing high quality feedback has long been recognized as one the most powerful instructional techniques for skill learning."
11. **NN/g's carousel research condemns auto-forwarding.** A Siemens Appliances U.K. homepage offer of "£100 off" sat on a carousel rotating **every 5 seconds**, so "the discount deal is visible only 20% of the time" and a user hunting for deals missed it. NN/g lists **six** problems: reduced accessibility for motor difficulties, insufficient reading time for low-literacy users, slower comprehension for non-native speakers, lower visibility from taking turns, loss of user control, and assumed advertising ("because it moves, users automatically assume that it might be an advertisement"). Recommendation verbatim: show a new panel "only when users ask for it. Otherwise, it should stand still and let users read the information in peace." Plus a directly usable dwell formula — "We use **3 words per second** as a guideline" — and "Do not auto-forward on mobile devices."
12. **WCAG 2.2 makes auto-advance expensive, and defaulting to manual makes the two hardest criteria inapplicable.** SC 2.2.2 (Level A) has two clauses, and **the five-second exception applies only to moving/blinking/scrolling, not to auto-updating**. SC 2.2.1 (Level A) requires that a content-set time limit be turn-off-able, adjustable "over a wide range that is at least ten times the length of the default setting", or extendable with a 20-second warning ten times over. **An auto-advance that removes a slide before the reader finishes is a content-set time limit**, so a compliant auto mode needs a **10× speed range**, not merely a pause button. There is a loophole — of a loading animation W3C writes "Because the moving content is not presented in parallel with other content, no mechanism to pause, stop or hide it needs to be provided" — **do not build on it.** It is a lawyer's argument, it evaporates the moment the viewer is embedded inline in a catalog page or an agent panel, and NN/g's usability findings apply regardless of conformance.
13. **Five more criteria bind the viewer.** SC 2.5.1 Pointer Gestures (A): swiping is a path-based gesture and needs a single-pointer alternative — and, verbatim, "keyboard operability is not sufficient to conform to this success criterion. That is because some users rely entirely on pointing devices" — **so a keyboard shortcut does not discharge the obligation; a visible button does.** SC 2.5.8 Target Size (AA): 24×24 CSS px minimum with five exceptions. SC 1.1.1 (A): the slide's caption is the *instruction*, not a description of the screenshot — **two different strings, both required**. SC 2.1.1 Keyboard (A). SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (AAA): sufficient techniques are **C39** and **SCR40** — it is the technique *using* `prefers-reduced-motion` that is sufficient, not passive support of the preference.
14. **The ARIA carousel pattern is prescriptive enough to implement verbatim.** Container `role=region|group` with `aria-roledescription="carousel"`; each slide `role="group"` with `aria-roledescription="slide"`; "If the rotation control is present, it is the first element in the Tab sequence inside the carousel"; the label swaps between "Stop slide rotation" and "Start slide rotation"; **"Automatic slide rotation stops when any element in the carousel receives keyboard focus. It does not resume unless the user activates the rotation control"**; it also stops on hover; activating any control does not move focus; the slide wrapper is `aria-atomic="false"` with `aria-live="off"` when rotating and `"polite"` when not. From W3C's tutorial, the trap that catches everyone: "The JavaScript only replaces the value of the `<button>` element and its attributes. **Replacing the entire button would result in loss of keyboard focus.**" *(Note: Techniques G4 and G186 are advisory — W3C states "Techniques are examples… They are not required to meet WCAG.")*
15. **Auto-generated step-guide tools split into three output shapes, and none auto-advances.** Tango produces static annotated screenshots ("Click through your workflows and Tango instantly creates a how-to guide with screenshots, highlights and descriptions you can customize") plus an in-app "Guide Me" overlay, and embeds "into your knowledge base, learning management, ticketing, and chat systems". Guidde produces AI video ("Powered by GPT", "over **200** different voices and languages"). Supademo produces interactive click-throughs where "viewers **click through steps at their own pace**, with branching logic and personalization". **RelayHQ's slides format sits on the Tango/Scribe shape — the cheapest to author, the cheapest to keep current, and reusable as prose. The market has converged on the answer the research supports.**
16. **The Instagram Stories interaction contract**, confirmed via a reimplementation rather than a Meta source: tap right advances, tap left returns, press-and-hold pauses, horizontal swipe moves between *story sets* not segments, and the segmented bar follows one rule — only the active segment animates 0→100%, prior full, later empty. The engineering point, verbatim: "A timer loop makes pause/resume and tap-to-skip trivial — you control the clock. **Pure CSS animations are harder to interrupt mid-fill.**"

**The design conclusion, and it is unusually well-supported:** build the Instagram **chrome** — segmented bar, tap zones, press-and-hold, full-bleed media — and make it **self-paced by default**, with auto-advance an opt-in the user turns on. Four independent lines converge and none supports auto-advance as a default. That is not a compromise; it is the higher-performing design, and it makes the accessibility spec nearly trivial. If auto mode is enabled, derive dwell from NN/g's verified figure: `clamp(4000, 20000, ceil(words(caption + expected) / 3) * 1000 + 1500) / speed`, with a speed set of 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 5 / 10 spanning a 20× range to clear SC 2.2.1. **On the last slide, auto mode stops. It never loops.**

**Not verified (content architecture).**

## 6. What we are deliberately NOT building

Recorded here so it is a roadmap rather than an omission, and so it does not get relitigated. Where any of these must appear for demo completeness, render them as **disabled menu items with a "Not in this prototype" tooltip** — a visibly deferred feature reads as a roadmap; a half-built one reads as a bug.

- **SCORM / AICC / xAPI package import and run-time, and an LRS endpoint.** A client-side prototype cannot host a run-time adapter and should not pretend. Adopt the *vocabulary* (cmi5 `moveOn`, the SCORM 2004 completion/success split, xAPI statement shape); implement none of the runtimes. If a prospect asks, the strong answer is that RelayHQ authors content natively rather than importing black boxes — which is exactly why the same atom can serve deflection and enablement, and a SCORM package can serve neither.
- **Audio narration, AI voiceover, and any on-screen presenter or avatar.** The evidence points against it in a self-paced viewer: modality's effect "was not obtained" when pace is slow and under learner control; redundancy (d = 0.72) penalises pairing narration with duplicate on-screen text, which is exactly what a captioned deck with voiceover is; and an on-screen instructor yields d = 0.26, below the practical-relevance bar. Guidde's AI-voiceover video (200+ voices) is the competitor shape and a self-paced captioned deck loses nothing to it while being far cheaper to author, translate, correct and keep current when the UI changes. Revisit only on real usage demand, and then as optional TTS *of the caption* — never separately authored audio that can drift from the text. If third-party video is ever embedded, require both a WebVTT captions track and a transcript, or the slide fails publish.
- **Auto-advance as a default.** Opt-in only, with a 10× speed range, a hard stop on the last slide, and no looping. See §4.4.
- **Block-level transclusion between atoms, conref push, multi-axis profiling, and forked structures.** Whole-atom reuse plus variables plus an audience filter. The honest position — "we reuse whole atoms and vary them only by token and audience" — is defensible, implementable, and fails in ways an author can see.
- **An if/then automatic-enrollment rule builder.** Job-function membership is the only automatic rule. The rule builder is the classic prototype tar pit and it demos worse than the coverage matrix. On the hard case — existing members when a curriculum changes — enrol new members going forward and require an explicit "Sync members" action to backfill, so nothing silently blasts assignments at people.
- **Monetisation, e-commerce, catalogue pricing, and proctored certification.** Skilljar leads with a commerce engine and Stripe; Thought Industries makes Monetize one of five pillars. That surface area is unwinnable client-side and irrelevant to a thesis about content topology. If gating must be shown, one field — `course.visibility: 'public'|'signed-in'|'entitled'` — and one lock glyph. No checkout, no cart, no plan comparison.
- **Leaderboards and daily streaks.** Add narrative track theming and cohort progress instead — game fiction and social interaction are the two moderators the independent meta-analysis actually names. **Be honest internally that the anti-leaderboard stance is design judgment, not a meta-analytic finding**; streaks in particular fit episodic B2B usage badly, where three weeks between logins is normal.
- **Question banks with draw-N randomization, per-lesson due dates, a credits/CEU system, ILT sessions and attendance, discussions/forums/ratings on courses, and approval workflows for enrollment.** All real deferrals — Litmos ships `discussion-forums`, `ratings-and-reviews` and `enrollment-approvals`; Moodle promoted `qbank` to a core activity module, which is a strong signal that pooling becomes non-optional at compliance scale. Due dates belong on the assignment, not the lesson. Credits stay out because any denominator mixing units is unpredictable to a learner. RelayHQ already has an approvals engine — reuse `approvalPolicyId` if enrollment approval is ever needed rather than building a second one.
- **Any borrowed deflection, adoption-lift, completion-benchmark or ROI statistic.** Not a single verified, disinterested figure exists in this entire research programme. RelayHQ's number must come from its own instrumented funnel, and the metric must be called **assisted resolution**, with the disclaimer printed next to it.
- **Relying on the WCAG SC 2.2.2 "not presented in parallel with other content" exemption.** It evaporates the moment the viewer is embedded inline in a catalog page or an agent panel, and the usability findings apply regardless of conformance.
